## **How Many Kilojoules Are Given Off When 17.8 mol of CH₄ React?**

### **Chemical Reaction:**
\[
\text{CH}_4(g) + 2\text{O}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{CO}_2(g) + 2\text{H}_2O(l) \quad \Delta H = -890.1 \, \text{kJ}
\]

---

## **Step-by-Step Friendly Explanation with Structure**

### **Step 1: Understanding What This Reaction Means**

Let’s break it down simply:

- **CH₄** is methane gas — think of it as natural gas used for cooking.
- **O₂** is oxygen gas from the air.
- They react (or “burn”) to produce:
  - **CO₂** (carbon dioxide)
  - **H₂O** (water)
- And as a **result**, the reaction **releases energy** — specifically **890.1 kJ** for **every 1 mole** of methane.

---

### **Visual: Mole Concept Diagram**

Here’s a simple diagram to help visualize the idea of moles and energy released:

```
            1 mol CH₄
           + 2 mol O₂
               |
         ------------------
         |                |
    1 mol CO₂        2 mol H₂O
               ↓
          -890.1 kJ (released)
```

**Think of this like a recipe**:  
If 1 cup of CH₄ makes one cake (with CO₂ and H₂O), and gives off 890.1 kJ of heat...  
then 17.8 cups (or moles) will give you 17.8 cakes worth of heat!

---

### **Step 2: What’s the Question Asking?**
It asks:
> If **17.8 moles** of CH₄ burn, how much heat is released?

---

### **Step 3: Use Simple Proportion**

We know:
- 1 mole of CH₄ → releases **890.1 kJ**
- So 17.8 moles of CH₄ → releases:  
\[
17.8 \times 890.1 = 15,842 \, \text{kJ}
\]

---

### **Extra Explanation: Why Multiply?**

A **mole** is just a count — like saying a “dozen.”  
So if **1 mole gives off 890.1 kJ**, then **17.8 moles give off 17.8 times more**.  
It’s **scaling up** the reaction.

---

### **Step 4: Final Answer**

\[
\boxed{15,\!842 \, \text{kJ of energy is released when 17.8 mol of CH₄ burns completely.}}
\]

---

## **Final Summary**

- Every 1 mole of CH₄ burns and gives off **–890.1 kJ**.
- Negative sign = energy is released (exothermic).
- 17.8 moles will release:  
  \[
  17.8 \times 890.1 = 15,\!842 \, \text{kJ}
  \]
- So the heat released = **15,842 kJ**

---

## **Helpful Tip for Students:**
When you see **ΔH = negative**, always remember:
> **"Negative = gives off heat" (exothermic)**  
This means the reaction is **hot**, like a fire or a stove.
